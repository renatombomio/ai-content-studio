# Story Specification

---

## Purpose

A Story exists to create emotional resonance through cinematic storytelling.

The objective is not to educate.
The objective is not to motivate.
The objective is not to give advice.

The objective is to make people feel understood.

---

## Story Structure

Every Story contains:

- **title** — the emotional core of the story in a few words.
- **hook** — the opening line that earns the next ten seconds.
- **scenes** — the sequence of visual and narrative moments.
- **caption** — the emotional residue left after the video ends.
- **hashtags** — a curated set of relevant tags.

---

## Duration

Target duration: **45–60 seconds.**

---

## Scenes

A Story should normally contain **6–10 scenes**.

Each scene must:

- Express one visual moment.
- Move the emotional narrative forward.
- Be easy to illustrate with video.

Narration should remain concise. Avoid unnecessary exposition. One strong image is worth more than three explanatory sentences.

---

## Hook

The first sentence must immediately create curiosity.

Never use clickbait.
Never promise an answer.
Never exaggerate.

The hook invites the viewer into the emotional world of the story. It does not describe what the story is about. It opens a door.

---

## Emotional Arc

The story should follow this general movement:

```
Recognition
    ↓
Conflict
    ↓
Reflection
    ↓
Resonance
```

Do not force explicit conclusions. The arc is felt, not announced. The viewer arrives at resonance because the story earns it — not because the narration points at it.

---

## Visual Language

Every line of narration must naturally suggest a visual.

The reader should be able to imagine every scene without any additional description. If a sentence cannot be seen, it should be rewritten or removed.

---

## Caption

The caption is not a summary of the story.

It is emotional residue — the feeling that lingers after the video ends. One or two sentences. Sometimes a fragment. It speaks to the heart of the story without retelling it.

---

## Hashtags

Three to five hashtags. Relevant to the story and the Cocoa Talk audience. Never padded. Never generic. Each hashtag should belong there.

---

## Ending

Do not explain the message.
Do not teach.
Do not solve the conflict.

Leave space for the viewer to complete the story in their own life. The most powerful endings are the ones that open something rather than close it.

---

## Quality Checklist

Before a Story is considered complete, verify:

- [ ] Feels emotionally honest.
- [ ] Can be visualized scene by scene.
- [ ] Contains no clichés.
- [ ] Contains no motivational language.
- [ ] Contains no fake wisdom.
- [ ] Respects Cocoa Talk identity.
- [ ] Every sentence justifies its existence.
- [ ] Removing one third of the words would not improve it.

---

A Story should not try to sound profound.

It should simply feel true.
